# "this is CINEMATOGRAPHY" — PloBoom (1:32)
A wordless music supercut of modern blockbuster frames (Nolan, Villeneuve-style imagery: Dunkirk beach, Interstellar's black hole, Oppenheimer). The "product" is the craft of cinematography itself. Whisper found no speech.

## Connections
- **Silhouette motif**: almost every frame has a small, backlit human figure, which turns 40 sources into one film.
- **Graphic matches**: horizon line, centre-framed subject and figures walking away keep the same screen position across cuts.
- Cuts land on the music grid, and shot length follows the music's intensity.
- Fire and orange light come back as callbacks (ruins, crucifix, explosion).

## Hook
A perfectly symmetrical, saturated corridor with one figure: instant "this is cinema" signalling and a strong vanishing point. Then the dead black beat at 4 s builds anticipation.

## Product / CTA
None. The ending is a held human close-up, then black: emotion is the sign-off.

## Pacing
60 cuts in 92 s = 61 shots, about 1.5 s average. The rhythm is a wave: slow (0:07–0:22) → fast (0:22–0:40, ~0.6 s) → slow → fast (1:03–1:21) → hold.

## Visual language
2.39:1 letterbox, teal-orange and monochrome-amber grades, natural or practical backlight, a lot of negative space, wide lenses, mostly locked or slow-moving camera. No typography.

## Sound
A single score with a steady loudness ramp (−24 → −10 LUFS) and no SFX layer. The music does all the structural work.

## Top 5 transferable techniques
1. A recurring small human silhouette as the visual through-line.
2. An intensity wave (slow-fast-slow-fast) instead of a constant pace.
3. A dead black beat right after the hook.
4. Letterbox and a strict composition grammar (centred, symmetric) as the "cinematic" signal.
5. End on a held, direct-to-camera face, not a montage.
