# Sound method — "The Power of Sound in Movies" (Paul E.T., 9:30, 2022)
Source: youtube.com/watch?v=iUov13NgU8w + lafilm.edu/blog/the-importance-of-sound. Demo: same 40 s forest short shown raw (1:48) then fully designed (7:35). Neither source gives EQ/compression/dB settings; items marked *[std]* are standard practice, not from the sources.

## Layers and their jobs
- **Dialogue/VO** — carries story; capture on set (performance can't be recreated in ADR); record breaths/efforts too (3:39).
- **Foley** — body-synced sound (steps, cloth, handling props), recorded after the shoot and synced (0:22).
- **Hard SFX** — discrete designed hits (ice crack, door creak); the source uses absurd props (fridge, bottle) — the sound only has to *look* right.
- **Ambience/room tone** — sets place and realism ("city or rural" — LAFS); replaces wind-ruined production audio (6:32, 7:12 adds ambience + bushes).
- **Music** — mood. Score/licensed library cut to picture vs needle drop of a famous movie track (the 2012 "cringe" mistake, 4:04).
- **Silence** — *[std]* drop the bed before a key line or title card so the hit lands.

## Grammar
Diegetic (forest, steps, voice) vs non-diegetic (score, risers). Perspective: level/reverb scale with distance *[std]*. Sound bridges: J-cut (next scene's audio first), L-cut (audio trails over cut) *[std]*. Motif: LAFS cites *Jaws* — two notes become the threat; reuse one sonic signature for the product.

## Build order (as shown)
1. Watch picture mute-raw; diagnose (1:48–2:31: wind only, "hip-hop B-roll").
2. Dialogue/VO + breaths first (3:39).
3. Music: filter by genre, mood, BPM, key; pick 1–2 tracks (5:04). Re-edit on bar lines — cut/move sections to extend or shorten (5:25).
4. Foley: perform props in a dead room (pine cone, twig crate, footsteps — frames 6:52–7:10).
5. Sync every hit by hand — slowest step, "worth your time" (7:12).
6. Ambience + texture beds (wind-free forest, bushes).
7. Mix and full playback (7:32); *[std]* duck music −6…−10 dB under VO, EQ-carve 2–4 kHz, short reverb matched to space, whoosh into cuts, impact on title cards ("SHADOW" stings).
Measured (ebur128 on the upload): raw cut −29.6 LUFS, LRA 21.8, peak −8.4 dBFS; finished −19.8 LUFS, LRA 12.3, peak −1.6 dBFS — design added ~10 LU and halved dynamic range.

## Free/cheap tools
Musicbed (sponsor, paid); "semi-decent mic + patience"; household props; Premiere timeline. LAFS: none.

## Mistakes
Needle-dropping copyrighted film music; trusting on-set audio full of wind; ADR when a live take was possible; un-synced or "wrong-looking" sounds (LAFS); recording near fans, A/C hum, neighbours.

## Critic checklist (measurable)
1. Integrated loudness −14 ±1 LUFS (web) / −24 (broadcast); true peak ≤ −1 dBTP.
2. LRA 6–12 LU — neither flat nor 20+ LU raw.
3. VO ≥ 8 dB above music under every line.
4. Every on-screen contact (step, touch, prop) has a foley/SFX within ±2 frames.
5. Ambience bed present on ≥ 90% of runtime; no dead digital silence unless intentional.
6. No wind/handling/hum in any dialogue segment (spectral check <150 Hz).
7. ≥ 1 deliberate silence/drop (≥ 0.5 s) before the key reveal.
8. Music edits land on bar lines; ending resolves on a downbeat, not a fade-cut mid-phrase.
9. Each title/logo card has an impact or sting.
10. ≥ 1 J- or L-cut across scene changes.
11. A recurring sonic motif (≤ 3 notes or one signature sound) appears ≥ 2 times, incl. the logo.
12. Music licensed/original — no recognizable film track.
13. Perspective: distant shots audibly quieter/wetter than close-ups.
14. Risers/whooshes ≤ 1 per 5 s; no stacked transitions.
