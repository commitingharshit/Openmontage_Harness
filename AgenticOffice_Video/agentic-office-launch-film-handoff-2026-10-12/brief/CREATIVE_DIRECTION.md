# New visual direction — production brief

This is a proposed visual upgrade, not a new product story or a completed film. Use Claude to plan/direct and OpenArt for controlled abstract motion assets. Composite authentic UI and exact typography in the chosen editor. Do not ask a generative video model to recreate our interface.

## Audience and feeling

A nontechnical consultant or lawyer should understand the offer in the first ten seconds: documents, spreadsheets, and presentations in **one professional workspace**, with their team and agents. Emotional arc: fragmentation → relief → capability → trust → momentum. Express it with spacing, rhythm, motion, and clear results—not generated faces or office scenes.

## Visual grammar

Preserve the opening's best idea: three work cards have identity, then combine into one workspace. Reuse those identities throughout rather than introduce unrelated visual styles every scene. Word = document lines, Excel = grid/data bars, PowerPoint = slide/chart; names and format marks are editor-owned, not generated text. Don't use official app logos unless approved assets/rights are supplied.

Baseline palette: paper `#F5F8F8`, ink/deep teal `#132D34`, teal `#066D82`, ice `#75D6E5`, muted `#42656D`, line `#A6C8CE`; sparing orange `#E26332`. Inter Regular/Bold is included. Carry this palette into every OpenArt prompt; use one material language (clean rounded planes, soft contact shadows, subtle translucent borders), not neon/cyberpunk/glitch collage.

Alternate a spacious light stage with a deep-teal stage at decisive beats. UI should be front-facing for reading, with brief restrained perspective during arrivals/transitions. Background parallax/bloom can suggest depth; do not blur text viewers need to read or distort UI aspect ratio.

Typography is the editor's layer: one thought at a time, strong hierarchy, short attack → readable hold → motivated exit. Feature headings are concise. Do not put a full paragraph in motion while the viewer must read the UI. Baseline 1080p composition uses roughly 110px side margins; protect the UI and all qualifiers in smaller/mobile playback.

## Choreography

- Use movement to connect cause and result: source files gather when the prompt references them; the deck arrives where the user can actually work on it.
- Reuse a continuous frame/viewport across the opening and reveal. Match position/scale between shots so transitions feel like one work system, not a slide deck.
- Focus on one action: composer → output; diff → decision; teammate → shared context; agent → handoff. Avoid simultaneous fast movement everywhere.
- Allow brief holds for the app names, prompt, output, Accept/Reject, and “You review. You decide.” Fill dead time with meaningful micro-motion, not arbitrary spinning/particle noise.
- Show the full workspace before punch-ins, then re-establish it. Viewers must know all features belong to the same product.
- Agent packets and team graphics are editorial overlays, not invented controls. Label illustrations/prototypes where needed.

## Audio production

Voice is mandatory. Existing Kokoro `af_heart` stems are included as timing/reference assets, not proof of a professionally recorded final VO. For a new voice, prefer warm, confident, conversational delivery with clear app names and emphasis on “one workspace,” “your agent,” and “you decide.” Obtain and check usage rights for any final voice service/recording.

Keep an original or explicitly licensed jazz-funk groove, snare/drum-roll builds, tasteful bass and rhythmic accents. The included sample-free score is 100 BPM. Current major beats: 5.4s reveal, 18.6s prompt, 24.6s deck, 43.8s agent handoff, 58.2s close. These are an existing edit map; re-lock hits to the final voice/music if changed. Do not reuse the YouTube recording without permission.

SFX should describe actions: a light card tick, soft movement whoosh, confirmation for a proven proposal/output, and a restrained reveal impact. Avoid noisy repeated clicks under every word. The four inherited SFX require provenance/license clearance before release.

Mix speech first; automate/duck the bed and carve masking frequencies based on the actual recorded stems. Existing review master measured −16.02 LUFS integrated and −1.49 dBTP; new mix target can stay around −16 LUFS / ≤−1.5 dBTP. Check on speakers and earbuds. Don't cut app-name breaths to meet a beat.
