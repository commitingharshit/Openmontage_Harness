# Motion reference — what to borrow

User's reference: [Best AI SaaS Product Launch Video | Numtera — ObiN Studio](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=awUYikrGsKk).

Inspected in the browser on 10 October 2026 by sampling opening and feature moments. This was not a complete frame-by-frame or audio analysis. The reference footage/audio is not included, downloaded, or licensed for our film.

Observed visual devices:

- Opening: large typography on a white field, with small rounded interface panels floating around it. Clear separation between the hero thought and supporting detail.
- Around 20s: cropped product interface, restrained perspective/depth, blue/light bloom around the panel. The UI is the proof, not a photographic environment.
- Around 35s: a focused ticket UI with a visible analysis/progress treatment. It connects an input to processing/result. We can borrow the causal clarity, not that product's “learning” claim or progress number.
- Later sampled UI scenes: tight input/detail views and a wider interface re-establishing shot, with subtle atmospheric depth. Exact later timestamp alignment was not reliable enough to use as an edit specification.

## Full-length pass (Claude, 10 October 2026)

Seek-sampled in the browser every 2–2.5 s across all 95 s, plus denser samples at transitions. Picture only; audio was not analysed. Nothing was downloaded.

| Time | Beat | Motion device |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–8 | Problem: "The same issue, Different channels" / "Same investigation… again?" | Typewriter line with caret on white; a text-selection highlight box sweeps over the key phrase; faded UI fragments (menus, ticket cards, named cursors) drift in at the edges and frame the line |
| 8–12 | Pivot: "Stop / Stop Managing tickets" then "Meet Numtera" | Word scales toward camera with motion blur and a gradient fill; push-through into a letter as a scale-through cut to a blue gradient field; next line builds word by word, brand word in accent colour |
| 13–17 | Positioning line | One centred line built word by word; one accent phrase; soft blue/white mesh gradient |
| 17–22 | Product reveal | Full UI enters in 3D perspective and drifts, short headline top-right, then flattens front-facing; push into a detail; isolated UI cards float above a soft background |
| 22–29 | Mechanism | Hard split wipe, light UI on the left and dark on the right, joined by a connector node; monospace checklist types in with ✓ and a progress bar |
| 30–39 | Kinetic metaphor | Line "Agent Approves it as { learning }" with code brackets; the bracketed word morphs into a progress bar, which zooms out to sit on a UI card; log lines type in beneath |
| 40–52 | Dark section | Dark-blue gradient; "[ knowledge ]" brackets wrap a scrambling word; UI flyover; one huge word ("Auto-resolved") and its payoff line |
| 54–75 | Second feature | Sidebar isolated with a cursor; macro close-ups of form fields with shallow depth of field; steps stack into a numbered timeline (1, 2, 3); click "Save"; UI tilts away and exits |
| 79–84 | Payoff | Two-line contrast ("They close tickets / We Eliminate them"), word by word with focus-pull blur |
| 84–95 | Brand and CTA | Icon then letter-by-letter wordmark build; vertical bar wipe to black; typewriter tagline; CTA and URL; fade out |

What makes it feel premium: almost every frame is either one line of type or one piece of UI, never both competing. There's a single accent colour, and backgrounds are simple gradients rather than imagery. Motion is mostly scale, blur and perspective, with hard light↔dark contrast cuts at section changes. Real UI is shown as isolated components and macro close-ups, not only as full screenshots.

Implication for OpenArt: this style is almost entirely typography, real UI and gradients built in the editor. Generated video plates are optional here, not core.

## Adaptation for Agentic Office

Keep the depth, scale changes, whitespace, floating panels, and focused action → result. Make our three-format merge the signature visual. Use teal instead of copying Numtera's blue identity. Integrate our real composer, output, diff, and collaboration panels. Use short UI-focused moves, then holds long enough to understand the work.

Do not copy the reference's brand, exact design, demo content, claims, footage, soundtrack, or pacing shot-for-shot. Our story is one professional workspace with people and agents—not a self-learning customer-support product.
